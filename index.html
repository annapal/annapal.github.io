About

I'm an environmental epidemiologist at Columbia University studying how heat, floods, droughts, and cyclones affect health during the two life stages when we are most vulnerable: childhood and older age.

Most of my work to date has focused on childhood, answering questions like: Do extreme weather events influence rates of child marriage around the world? Does flooding affect whether a child ever starts school?

Now I'm working on the other end of life, examining how temperature shapes aging and dementia, from mapping dementia deaths across US counties to testing whether heat stress injures the brain.

My love of nature is what drew me to this field, and I try to spend as much time outdoors as I can (hiking and rock climbing).
